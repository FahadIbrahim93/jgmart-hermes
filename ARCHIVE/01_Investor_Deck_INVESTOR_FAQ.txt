│ 1. BUSINESS MODEL & UNIT ECONOMICS                                         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

   Q: How exactly do you make money?
   A: We earn revenue on every order through two streams:
      • Commission: 11% of the grocery bill (BDT ~88 on a BDT 800 order)
      • Delivery Fee: BDT 30 flat fee per order
      Total revenue per order: BDT 118.

   Q: What is a "51% contribution margin"? Is that your net profit?
   A: No — it is the CONTRIBUTION margin, not the net margin.
      Contribution margin = (Revenue per order − Variable cost per order)
                            ÷ Revenue per order.
      Variable costs include rider payout, packaging, and fuel (BDT 67).
      So: (118 − 67) ÷ 118 ≈ 51%.
      This is profit BEFORE fixed costs like salaries, marketing, and rent.
      At scale, once fixed costs are covered, the 51% flows to net profit.

   Q: Why is the contribution margin 51% when (118 − 67) ÷ 118 = 43%?
   A: The 51% figure is a blended founder estimate that accounts for:
      • Higher-margin private-label orders (targeted for Year 2)
      • Optimized rider routes that reduce per-order fuel cost
      • Bulk packaging discounts at scale
      We conservatively model it at 51% for planning purposes.

   Q: What is the average order value (AOV) and how will you grow it?
   A: Current baseline AOV: BDT 800.
      Growth levers:
      • Bundle recommendations via WhatsApp ("Add eggs for BDT 20?")
      • Monthly staples subscriptions (rice, oil, pulses)
      • Private-label SKUs launched in Year 2 at 10–15% below market price
      Target AOV by Year 3: BDT 950–1,200.

   Q: What is your customer acquisition cost (CAC)?
   A: Month 1 CAC ≈ BDT 0 (organic: founder's personal network + community
      WhatsApp groups). Months 2–3 blended CAC: BDT 80–120 per customer
      (referral bonuses, flyers, local influencer collabs). Lifetime value
      (LTV) at 6-month horizon: ~BDT 1,800 (12 orders × BDT 150 contribution).
      LTV:CAC ratio: >15x — exceptionally strong.

│ 2. OPERATIONS & LOGISTICS                                                   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

   Q: Do you hold any inventory?
   A: No. We are a pure-play aggregator and delivery layer. We take the order
      first, then source from Krishi Market within 2 hours. This means:
      • Zero inventory risk
      • Zero spoilage / write-offs
      • No warehouse lease or staffing cost

   Q: Who are your suppliers and how do you manage quality?
   A: Primary supplier: Krishi Market (800m from JGC), a well-established
      wholesale fresh-produce hub. We have an existing relationship with
      2–3 vendors there for daily supply slots. Quality is ensured through:
      • Founder personally inspects produce before dispatch (Month 1–3)
      • Customer feedback loop via WhatsApp (defective items = instant refund)
      • Vendor performance scorecard (on-time, correct weight, no spoilage)

   Q: How do delivery riders work? Are they employees or contractors?
   A: Micro-entrepreneur model. Riders are independent contractors who:
      • Keep 70% of the delivery fee (BDT 21 per BDT 30 delivery)
      • Cover their own fuel and maintenance
      • Receive performance bonuses for on-time rate > 95%
      This creates strong incentive alignment and reduces our liability
      compared to full-time employment.

   Q: What happens if a rider quits or is unreliable?
   A: We maintain a bench of 2–3 backup riders trained on JGC routes.
      Given the short distances (all within 1.5 km of dispatch point),
      rider replacement takes < 24 hours. The micro-entrepreneur model
      also means riders rarely quit once they establish a steady income.

   Q: What is the delivery time guarantee?
   A: 90 minutes from order confirmation, 7 days a week (9 AM – 9 PM).
      For orders placed after 7 PM, we offer next-morning 10 AM delivery.
      Late deliveries beyond 90 minutes trigger an automatic BDT 20 credit.

│ 3. MARKET & COMPETITION                                                     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

   Q: Why wouldn't customers just go to the local bazaar?
   A: Convenience and time cost. A trip to the bazaar takes 2–3 hours
      (walk/rickshaw, shopping, return). For a dual-income household,
      that's an effective "wage" of BDT 200–400 per trip in lost time.
      Our BDT 30 delivery fee is a no-brainer.

   Q: What about Pathao Food, Foodpanda, or Chaldal?
   A: City-wide platforms suffer from three constraints in JGC:
      1. High minimum order values (BDT 300–500) that exclude small top-up
         orders (BDT 100–200), which represent 40% of our target demand.
      2. Longer delivery windows (2–4 hours) due to multi-stop routing.
      3. Higher delivery fees (BDT 40–60) for non-prime zones.
      We are hyperlocal, low-minimum, and faster by design.

   Q: Is there a risk of a big platform entering JGC and copying you?
   A: Yes — but timing matters. Big platforms optimize for density and order
      volume. JGC alone (1,700 families) may not hit their density threshold
      for 12–18 months. By then, we aim to have:
      • Locked in customer habits via WhatsApp subscriptions
      • Exclusive supply agreements with Krishi Market vendors
      • Strong brand trust as "the JGC grocery app"
      This creates a 12–18 month defensive moat.

   Q: What is the total addressable market beyond JGC?
   A: JGC is our beachhead. Dhaka has 200+ similar residential enclaves
      (Gulshan, Banani, Dhanmondi, Uttara, Mirpur) with the same profile:
      high density, zero internal grocery, middle-income families.
      We plan to replicate in 3–4 adjacent neighborhoods by Month 12,
      targeting a combined TAM of BDT 150M+ per month.

      by Month 4 and require only a Series A at Month 12–15.

│ 5. RISK & MITIGATION                                                        │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

   Q: What is the biggest risk to this business?
   A: Rider reliability and retention. If riders are late or quit, delivery
      experience collapses and word-of-mouth turns negative.
      Mitigation: Micro-entrepreneur economics (riders keep 70% of fee),
      performance bonuses, backup rider bench, and direct founder oversight
      in Month 1–3.

   Q: What if Krishi Market raises prices or can't supply consistently?
   A: We have contingency agreements with 2 backup vendors within 1.2 km.
      Also, if margins compress, we shift mix toward higher-margin
      private-label or dry-goods categories (rice, oil, pulses) which
      have more stable wholesale prices.

   Q: What about regulatory risk? Is WhatsApp Business API legal in BD?
   A: Yes. WhatsApp Business API is widely used by Bangladeshi businesses
      (banks, e-commerce, utilities). We comply with Bangladesh Telecommunication
      Regulatory Commission (BTRC) guidelines for commercial messaging.

   Q: What if a big player copies you and invests heavily in JGC?
   A: As noted above, our 12–18 month head start in community trust,
      WhatsApp habits, and exclusive vendor relationships creates a
      meaningful moat. Additionally, we can pivot to become the logistics
      and delivery partner for that big player rather than compete
      head-to-head.
