PART 1: THE 3-MINUTE PITCH BREAKDOWN
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

SECONDS 00-30: THE PROBLEM (Hook)
  "Japan Garden City has 1,700 families and ZERO grocery stores.
  Every single day, families lose 2-3 hours getting groceries.
  My brother-in-law spends more time in traffic than with his kids.
  This is a problem I've lived for 8 years."

  🎯 TONE: Personal, relatable, from the heart
  🚫 AVOID: Stats without the human story

SECONDS 30-60: THE SOLUTION (2 sentences + model)
  "JG Mart delivers fresh groceries from Krishi Market in 90 minutes.
  No warehouse. No inventory. No app download.
  Customers order on WhatsApp — already installed on every phone.
  We batch orders, pick up from partners 800m away, and deliver."

SECONDS 60-90: UNIT ECONOMICS (THE MONEY)
  "Here's the math investors love: per order —"
  "Revenue: 138 BDT. Cost to serve: 67 BDT."
  "Contribution: 71 BDT. 51% margin."
  "Month one: 100 customers, 300 orders. Month three: 350, 1650."
  "Break-even by month three."

  🎯 TONE: Confident, practiced, numbers off the top of your head
  💡 KEY: Say it like you've said it 100 times

SECONDS 90-150: THE ASK + USE OF FUNDS
  "I'm raising 250,000 BDT — that's 2.9 thousand dollars.
  72K for rider salary. 72K for ops support. 50K marketing.
  50K working capital. 6K for equipment."
  "At our burn rate, this gives us 4-5 months of runway.
  We'll be profitable by month three."

SECONDS 150-180: THE CLOSE (Invitation)
  "I'm not asking you to fund a fantasy.
  Krishi Market is 800 meters from JGC. I've walked it 200 times.
  I have 5 beta customers ready. My brother is the rider.
  I need a partner who believes that the best businesses solve
  real problems for real neighbours.
  I'd love to walk you through the detailed numbers."

  🎯 TONE: Confident, humble, determined
  🚫 AVOID: Begging or sounding desperate

PART 2: THE 10 MOST LIKELY OBJECTIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

OBJECTION 1: "Isn't this too small for VC?"
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

THE WRONG ANSWER:
  "No, we'll scale to 10,000 customers in a year..."

THE RIGHT ANSWER:
  "You're right — 250K BDT is not a VC-sized check.
  That's exactly the point. We're not asking for a big bet.
  We're asking for a small bet on a model that WORKS without
  burning millions.
  If we prove 350 customers in JGC at 51% contribution margin,
  we unlock a repeatable playbook. Same model works in Gulshan,
  Banani, Uttara. That's when the real conversation starts.
  For now, this is a capital-efficient proof of concept."

OBJECTION 2: "Chaldal will crush you once they enter Mohammadpur"
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

THE WRONG ANSWER:
  "We're different from Chaldal..."

THE RIGHT ANSWER:
  "Chaldal is a $30M warehouse model. We're a 1-man WhatsApp service.
  When Chaldal enters Mohammadpur, they need a 10,000 sq ft warehouse,
  50 staff, and 15 vans. They'll spend 15 lakh/month before their
  first delivery.
  I operate from my phone. I source from a market 800m away.
  Chaldal's fastest delivery is 3 hours. Mine is 90 minutes.
  Most importantly: Chaldal is an app. I'm a neighbour.
  When a resident messages me, they get a person they've seen
  in the lift. You can't replicate that with an algorithm."

OBJECTION 3: "Only 300 orders in Month 1? That's nothing."
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

THE WRONG ANSWER:
  "We'll actually do 500..."

THE RIGHT ANSWER:
  "300 orders is deliberately conservative.
  100 families × 3 orders/month = 3 orders per customer.
  That's 0.75 orders per WEEK.
  If each family spends 8,000 BDT/month on groceries,
  we're capturing just 2,400 BDT — that's 30% of their wallet.
  We don't need to capture 100%. We just need to be their
  go-to for daily essentials.
  At 51% contribution margin, 300 orders already proves
  unit economics. Month 2 shows the leverage: 200 customers
  but 900 orders (4.5/month each). That's word-of-mouth + habit."

OBJECTION 4: "Why not build an app like every other startup?"
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

THE RIGHT ANSWER:
  "Building an app would cost 5-10 lakh and take 4 months.
  WhatsApp is on 95% of Bangladeshi phones and I can start
  TODAY.
  The app is a feature, not a moat. We're starting with
  the highest-friction channel so when we DO build an app,
  we already have customers, processes, and trust.
  Flip the question: why waste money on an app when you
  haven't proven anyone wants delivery yet?"

OBJECTION 5: "What happens if your brother gets sick?"
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

THE RIGHT ANSWER:
  "Good question. I've planned for this:
  Phase 1 (Month 1): My brother is primary. I'm backup.
  Phase 2 (Month 2): We hire a backup rider — part time.
  Cost: 6,000 BDT/month. At 900 orders, contribution is
  63,900. 6K is easily absorbed.
  Phase 3 (Month 3+): We have 2 full-time riders.
  Key metric: rider cost as % of revenue caps at 35%.
  Even at 2 riders, we're well within that."

OBJECTION 6: "How do you handle COD fraud or theft?"
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

THE RIGHT ANSWER:
  "Two simple controls:
  1. My brother handles all cash. No exceptions.
  2. Every order gets a printed receipt with customer signature.
  Month 1 target is 100 customers known to me personally.
  Brand-new riders won't handle cash alone until we have
  a track record and a small security deposit system.
  bKash payments leave a digital trail, so no risk there.
  In Bangladesh, bKash is preferred for 60%+ of transactions.
  We'll actively push bKash over COD."

OBJECTION 7: "250K is too much / too little for what you're doing"
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

THE RIGHT ANSWER (if "too much"):
  "250K covers 6 months of rider and manager salaries, plus
  marketing and a buffer. The average delivery startup in Dhaka
  burns 12 lakh/month. We're asking for 2.5 lakh total.
  That's not too much — that's capital-efficient."

THE RIGHT ANSWER (if "too little"):
  "You're right that 250K won't build an empire.
  But it will prove the unit economics. Once I show 350 customers
  at 51% margin, the next round is easy.
  I'd rather ask for too little and prove discipline than ask
  for too much and waste it."

OBJECTION 8: "Krishi Market prices are volatile. How do you handle?"
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

THE RIGHT ANSWER:
  "We update our prices daily based on morning Krishi Market rates.
  Customers see TODAY'S price, not a static menu.
  Our commission model protects us: we charge 11% of the actual
  price. If prices go up, our absolute commission goes up too.
  We also offer substitutions: if potato is expensive, we suggest
  alternatives before checkout.
  The volatility is a feature, not a bug — it proves we're
  connected to the real market."

OBJECTION 9: "This doesn't scale beyond JGC"
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

THE RIGHT ANSWER:
  "JGC is the prototype. There are 80+ residential enclaves
  in Dhaka with the exact same problem: no grocery store,
  bazaar is 1+ km away, residents have cars but not time.
  Once we prove the JGC playbook, we:
  1. Hire a local ops lead in the next enclave
  2. Replicate the partnership model with that area's bazaar
  3. Centralize management (WhatsApp number, accounting, QA)
  Each enclave is an independent P&L. Total SG&A for 5 enclaves
  is only ~3-4x a single enclave's overhead because management
  is centralized."

OBJECTION 10: "Why should I invest NOW? Why not wait 6 months?"
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

THE RIGHT ANSWER:
  "If you wait 6 months, you'll see one of two things:
  1. We succeed — and I'll raise at 5x the valuation.
  2. We fail — and you'll know exactly why.
  Investing NOW means you get in at a 1 Crore valuation cap.
  If we hit 350 customers at 51% margin, the next round is
  at 5-10 Crore.
  Your risk: 250K BDT. Your upside: 10x in 12-18 months.
  If I'm wrong, I'll tell you within 90 days with hard data."

PART 3: THE "TRAP" QUESTIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

These questions are designed to test you, not gather information.
If you stumble, they lose confidence.

TRAP 1: "What's your gross margin?"
  WHAT THEY'RE TESTING: Do you know your numbers?
  ✗ WRONG: "Um, I think it's around 50%..."
  ✓ RIGHT: "Our contribution margin is 51% — that's 71 BDT per order
    after variable costs but before fixed overhead. Our net margin
    hits 4.5% in Month 3 and scales from there."

TRAP 2: "What keeps you up at night?"
  WHAT THEY'RE TESTING: Do you understand your biggest risk?
  ✗ WRONG: "Nothing, I'm super confident..."
  ✓ RIGHT: "Two things: speed of adoption and rider reliability.
    If we don't hit 100 customers in Month 1, we don't hit 200
    in Month 2. That's why marketing and referrals are 20% of
    our ask — customer acquisition is our single biggest variable."

TRAP 3: "Who else have you pitched?"
  WHAT THEY'RE TESTING: Social proof / desperation
  ✗ WRONG: "You're the first person. I've been rejected 15 times..."
  ✓ RIGHT: "I've shared this with a few people — an ex-Chaldal
    manager provided input on our model and an FMCG investor
    is reviewing the terms. I wanted to bring you a refined
    version rather than a rough draft."

TRAP 4: "Tell me about your team"
  WHAT THEY'RE TESTING: Are you a one-person show?
  ✗ WRONG: "It's just me and my brother..."
  ✓ RIGHT: "I'm the founder — I handle operations, partnerships,
    and customer acquisition. My brother is my ops lead — he
    does morning pickups and deliveries. We're exploring an
    advisory board that includes an ex-Chaldal category manager.
    I've also built a network of 5 JGC building committee
    members who act as community ambassadors."

TRAP 5: "What's your exit strategy?"
  WHAT THEY'RE TESTING: Do you think long-term?
  ✗ WRONG: "We want to IPO..."
  ✓ RIGHT: "In 24-36 months, we're most likely an acquisition target
    for Chaldal, Foodpanda, or Daraz Grocery. A hyperlocal delivery
    network in Dhaka's premium enclaves is a strategic asset — it
    saves them 3-4 years of building local partnerships. If that
    doesn't materialize, we've built a profitable cash-flow
    business with 50%+ margins. Either outcome works for investors."

PART 5: BODY LANGUAGE & TONE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

DO:
  ✓ Lean forward slightly — shows engagement
  ✓ Keep hands visible — builds trust
  ✓ Nod when they're talking — shows listening
  ✓ Pause before answering hard questions — shows thought
  ✓ Use hand gestures when talking about JGC (point)
  ✓ Smile when talking about your customers

DON'T:
  ✗ Cross your arms — defensive
  ✗ Look at your phone or watch — disrespectful
  ✗ Talk too fast when nervous — slow down
  ✗ Say "honestly" — implies you're usually not
  ✗ Fill silence with um/uh/like
  ✗ Apologize for anything

VOICE CADENCE:
  "Japan Garden City has 1,700 families... and ZERO grocery stores."
  (pause after 1,700, emphasis on ZERO)
  "Revenue: 138. Cost: 67. Contribution: 71. 51% margin."
  (say it fast and rhythmic like a trader)

PART 6: RED FLAGS INVESTORS WATCH FOR
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

FLAG 1 — You DON'T KNOW your numbers
  ❌ "Um, I think the margin is about 50%"
  ✅ "Contribution margin: 51%. 71 BDT per order."

FLAG 2 — You don't understand the BUSINESS MODEL
  ❌ "We're like Chaldal but better" (you're NOTHING like Chaldal)
  ✅ "We're a hyperlocal aggregator. We don't hold inventory."

FLAG 3 — You get DEFENSIVE about questions
  ❌ "Actually, that's wrong — we would never..."
  ✅ "Good question. Here's how we handle that..."

FLAG 4 — Your numbers don't ADD UP
  ❌ "Revenue is 240K month 1 and costs are 65K"
  ✅ (Make sure everything is consistent across all docs)

FLAG 5 — You don't have SKIN IN THE GAME
  ❌ "I need 250K to start this business"
  ✅ "I'm investing my own time, my brother is giving up
      his job, and I've already spent 15K of my own money
      on equipment and flyers."

FLAG 6 — You haven't TALKED TO CUSTOMERS
  ❌ "I know people want this because my neighbours say so"
  ✅ "I have 5 beta customers confirmed. 3 building committee
      members are ready to endorse the service."

PART 7: POST-PITCH PROTOCOL
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

WITHIN 15 MINUTES:
  ✓ Send a thank-you WhatsApp
  ✓ Share the investor 1-pager (PDF)
  ✓ Note any questions you couldn't answer — research them

WITHIN 24 HOURS:
  ✓ Follow up with any numbers they asked for
  ✓ Share a relevant data point (e.g., new beta signup)
  ✓ Offer to meet again at Krishi Market to see the operation

IF THEY SAY YES (interested):
  ✓ Share financial model
  ✓ Set up a follow-up call within 48 hours
  ✓ Prepare a simple term sheet
  ✓ Ask for introduction to their network

IF THEY SAY NO:
  ✓ Ask for specific feedback (WHY?)
  ✓ Ask if they know anyone else who might be interested
  ✓ Add them to your monthly update list
  ✓ Follow up in 3 months with traction that proves them wrong

IF THEY SAY "MAYBE" / "TALK TO ME LATER":
  ✓ Ask for a specific timeline (1 month? 3 months?)
  ✓ Add to your newsletter/update list
  ✓ Send monthly 1-paragraph progress updates
  ✓ Prove them right by showing traction

PART 8: PITCH PRACTICE DRILLS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

DRILL 1: THE MIRROR
  Stand in front of a mirror. Pitch for 3 minutes.
  Watch your own face. Do you look confident?
  Do it 5 times. Minimum.

DRILL 2: THE FRIEND
  Pitch to a non-business friend (your brother).
  If they understand it, anyone will.
  Get them to ask 5 random questions.

DRILL 3: THE METRONOME
  Set a timer for 3 minutes.
  Hit each section within the time.
  If you rush, you're leaving out context.
  If you finish early, you're skipping the close.

DRILL 4: THE RECORDING
  Record yourself on WhatsApp voice note.
  Listen back. Count the ums and uhs.
  Do it again until zero filler words.
